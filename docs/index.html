Do this first

Start here: your computer and your Claude account

Two checks before anything else. Both take minutes, and both are easier to do now than to discover halfway through your first campaign.

1

Make sure the computer you'll use is clean

This one protects the businesses you're about to contact, not just you.

This pilot puts real people's names and work email addresses on your computer, stays signed in to your Airtable and Gmail, and writes drafts from your own inbox. The planners on your list never agreed to trust your machine. If it is compromised, your outreach becomes their problem, and it lands on your reputation and ours.

  • ✓Install every pending update and restart if it asks. On a Mac this is also what keeps Apple's built-in malware protection current.
  • ✓Run a full virus scan and confirm it comes back clean. A full scan, not a quick one. On Windows: Windows Security → Virus & threat protection — check that real-time protection is on, then run the full scan from there.
  • ✓Look through your browser extensions and startup programs. Remove anything you do not recognize. This is where password-stealing software usually hides.
  • ✓Turn on disk encryption and a screen lock. BitLocker on Windows, FileVault on a Mac.
!Using a Mac? This still applies to you. The kind of malware that matters here goes after saved browser logins, keychain passwords, and API keys, and it is written for Macs too. The risk is the same; only the antivirus tool differs.
!If a scan has ever found something on this computer, treat every password and key as taken. From a different computer you trust: change your Google and Airtable passwords, sign out of all active sessions, and regenerate your keys. Signing sessions out is the part that actually locks an intruder out — changing a password alone does not.
◆If the Center for Rural AI is helping you set this up, we confirm this before we begin, not after.
2

Check your Claude account

Any paid plan works. One setting has to be right.

You need a Claude account with Projects. Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise all work — there is no need to upgrade for this. What does matter is one setting, because the pilot has to reach planners' websites to read their contact pages.

  • ✓Open Settings → Capabilities and set "Allow network egress" to "All domains." The narrower "package managers only" option is not enough — Step 1 and Step 3 visit ordinary business websites, which that setting blocks.
!On Pro or Max, this is already on by default and you can change it yourself. On Team or Enterprise, it is switched off by default and only an organization owner can turn it on, under Organization settings → Capabilities. If that is not you, this is the one thing to ask for before you start. Anthropic's guide to the setting.
Do this next

Then: get your three keys

Quick, free signups that happen outside Claude (the third only if you're targeting corporate retreat planners). They take the longest, so grab them before you start and you won't have to stop partway through. Copy each key somewhere handy, like a notes app.

S

Serper — required

Powers the planner search (used at Step 1).

  • 1Go to serper.dev and sign up — free, no credit card (Google sign-in works). You start with 2,500 free searches.
  • 2Open your dashboard and click API Key in the left sidebar.
  • 3Copy the key and keep it somewhere handy. Once your base exists, it goes in the Config table under serper-api-key, and Step 1 (Find planners) picks it up from there.
H

Hunter — recommended

Finds extra contact emails (used at Step 3, pass 2).

  • 1Go to hunter.io and create a free account (email or Google).
  • 2Click your profile icon (top right) and choose API — or go straight to hunter.io/api-keys.
  • 3Copy the key. Once your base exists, it goes in the Config table under hunter-api-key, and Step 3's pass 2 picks it up from there. The free plan has a small monthly limit — your dashboard shows how many searches you have left.
A

Apollo — for Corporate segment

Powers decision-maker research for corporate retreat planners (used by the optional corporate-research skill, not one of the 4 numbered steps below).

  • 1Go to apollo.io and create a free account — free tier is 75 credits/month, similar to Hunter's limit.
  • 2Click Admin Settings (bottom-left navigation) → Integrations. This opens the developer portal.
  • 3In the developer portal's left navigation, click Integrations → API Keys and generate one.
  • 4Copy the key and keep it somewhere handy. Once your base exists, it goes in the Config table under apollo-api-key, and the Apollo search picks it up from there. Skip this if you're only targeting weddings.
!Do this before you start — each signup takes a few minutes and sends you off to another site, so it's easier to knock them out now than to break your flow later.

Do once

Set up once

A few minutes the first time. After this, you only ever repeat steps 1–4.

Get the project ready

  • ✓Create a new project in Claude. Name it something clear like "Email Outreach." Everything below lives inside this project.
  • ✓Connect Airtable and Gmail. Settings → Connectors. This is where your list and your drafts live. Each one asks what Claude can access, and each asks differently. Airtable offers two choices: pick "Everything you have access to." Gmail shows a checklist: tick "Select all." Claude needs this much access to build your base and put drafts in your inbox.
  • ✓Download the skills, then add them. Go to the project page on GitHub, click the green Code button, choose Download ZIP, and unzip it. The files you need are in the install folder, one ".skill" file per skill. Then in Claude: Settings → Skills → Add Skill, and upload each one. They show up in every chat after that.
  • ✓Put your keys in the Config table. The Serper and Hunter keys from "Get your keys" (plus Apollo if you're running corporate-research). Once Claude has built your base in Step A, open it, go to the Config table, and paste each key into the cell under its heading. Do this once and every step afterwards finds its own key.
A

Tell Claude about your inn

Builds your workspace and saves your details.

Prompt
Onboard a new client.
Action
First Claude asks two multiple-choice questions, one after the other. Which segments do you run? is the kind of planners you want to reach: wedding, corporate retreat, or both. Do you already have an Airtable base? is asking whether you have done this before: your first time, pick the option to create one fresh. (The exact wording varies a little each run.) Then it interviews you about the property: name, location, how many guests you host, what makes it special, who signs the emails, and the airport guests fly into. Then it builds everything.
You approve
Just answer as they come — Claude asks one or two at a time, so there's nothing to fill in beforehand. Not sure on something? Say so and it'll help.
!One quick click it will ask for: in the Firms table, add a field named discovered-date of type Created time. Once, and you're done.
B

Capture your voice

So the emails sound like you, not a template.

Prompt
Capture the voice for my business and draft the outreach templates.
Action
Claude asks how you write, then drafts your email templates and shows them to you.
You approve
Read the drafts, ask for any tweaks, and approve. These become the emails you send later.
◆Corporate segment: you approve two letters here, not one. They go to two different kinds of reader. An agency plans other people's events for a living, so that letter can talk about their work booking groups. An in-house contact — the HR director or office manager Apollo finds in Step 2 — organizes maybe one retreat a year. Sending them the agency letter compliments them on a business they do not have, which reads as a mistake. Claude drafts both; read both. Wedding-only? One letter, and you are done.
C

Work out who books corporate retreats

Corporate segment only. Skip this if you are targeting weddings.

Prompt
Run corporate-research for my target markets.
Action
Claude works through who actually books retreats at a company: an employee inside the business (an HR director or office manager), an outside agency hired for it, or a mix. It saves what it finds to a Corporate Research table as decision-maker profiles: the job titles to target and the size of company to look at.
You approve
You are answering questions about your market, so there is nothing to prepare. Read the profiles at the end and say if they look wrong.
◆Do this before Step 1, not after. The profiles decide what Step 1 searches for, and they are the input Apollo needs later. Running discovery first still finds real agencies, it just searches without the benefit of the research.
Repeat anytime

Run a campaign

These four steps are the loop you run whenever you want a fresh batch of outreach — a new city, a new season, a new segment.

1

Find planners

Searches an area for the people who book stays.

Prompt
Run firm-discovery for [target city], [target segment], small sample.
Action
Claude finds the planners in that area and lists them. Nothing is saved yet.
You approve
Nothing — just look. Fill in the city and segment you're targeting. Keep small sample in the prompt: it tells Claude to pull just a first batch (about one page of firms) instead of a full run, so you can check quality and spend before going bigger.
!Before your first run, put your Serper key in Airtable rather than pasting it into the chat. Open your base, go to the Config table, and paste the key into the cell under the serper-api-key heading. Claude picks it up from there every run, so you only do this once. While you are in there you can fill in hunter-api-key and apollo-api-key too, using the keys from "Get your keys," and later steps will just work.
◆Corporate segment: put Corporate in the prompt where it says segment, and finish Card C first. Corporate searches three phrasings per city instead of the one wedding uses, so it spends about three times the Serper credits. That is normal, and the small sample wording keeps the first run cheap.
2

Keep the good ones

Sorts real planners from the noise and saves them.

Prompt
Review those discovery results and save the good ones to Firms.
Action
Claude decides which results are genuine planners worth keeping and saves those to your list. It cross-checks each name against Google's category, so a rental company or venue that slipped in gets filtered out.
You approve
Just the close calls. Claude saves the obvious planners on its own and brings you only the handful it isn't sure about, usually where the firm's name and Google's category disagree. Answer yes or no on those and it writes the rest.
◆Corporate segment: there is a second group of people to reach. Steps 1 and 2 find event planning agencies. They cannot find the HR director or office manager who books their own company's retreat, because those people have no listing to search. The prompt below goes after them: it uses the job titles from Card C, shows you the candidates it finds, and saves the ones you approve. Their email comes with them, so they skip Step 3 and go straight to Step 4.
Corporate only · in-house planners
Run the Apollo search for my corporate profiles.
3

Get contact info

Finds email addresses for the ones you kept.

Prompt
Run contact-extraction on the Firms — pass 1 (scrape).
Action
Claude reads each planner's website, pulls an email address where it can, and saves those straight into your Contacts table.
You approve
Nothing. The addresses are in Airtable as soon as this finishes, so they are safe even if you stop here. Want more coverage? Run the optional pass below and it fills in the gaps.
Optional · find more
Run pass 2 (Hunter enrichment).
!Pass 2 uses your Hunter key. If you put it in the Config table alongside the Serper one, Claude picks it up on its own and there is nothing to paste.
✓Pass 2 works on firms that have their own website domain, where it can fill in a missing address and put a real name to a generic "info@" one. Firms that use a Gmail address are already as complete as they are going to get, so a short pass 2 list usually means pass 1 did well.
◆Corporate segment: run this step as written. The agencies you kept in Step 2 have websites, so both passes work on them exactly as they do for wedding planners. The one difference is that anyone Apollo found already came with an email address, so this step has nothing to do for them and will pass over them.
4

Write the emails

One personalized draft per contact, in your Gmail.

Prompt
Create one Gmail draft per Contact — drafts only, do not send.
Action
Claude writes a tailored email to each contact — in your voice, with the right travel details — and saves them to your Gmail Drafts.
You approve
Move to the last step to review them.
✓Claude never sends anything. Every email waits in your Drafts until you send it yourself.
◆Corporate segment: each contact gets the letter written for their kind of reader — agency or in-house — picked automatically. If you skipped the in-house letter back in Card B, this step stops and tells you rather than sending the agency letter instead. Go back, approve the second letter, and run this again.
You're in control

Review & send

The final call is always yours.

  • 1Open Gmail → Drafts. Your new outreach emails are waiting there.
  • 2Read each one. Tweak anything you like — they're normal Gmail drafts.
  • 3Hit send on the ones you're happy with. Skip or delete any you don't want. Done.

Good to know

✓Start small. Run one city as a sample first. You'll see the quality before spending on a bigger search.
✓Setup is one-time. Cards A and B happen once for your business (plus C if you run the corporate segment). Steps 1–4 are what you repeat for each campaign.
!If a step stalls, just try it again, or reopen the project in a browser. It's usually a hiccup, not a real error.
✓Nothing goes out without you. The pilot only ever prepares drafts. You send from your own inbox.
